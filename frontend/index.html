Before You Fill: Heart Terms Guide

Simple meanings and common adult ranges. Use your latest lab/test values if available.

Age

How old you are in years.

Typical range: 18-100 years

How to know: Self-reported from date of birth (no device).

Sex

Biological sex used by this model.

Options: Male or Female

How to know: Self-reported clinical demographic detail.

What Is Angina?

Angina means chest pain or pressure when the heart muscle does not get enough oxygen-rich blood, often during activity or stress.

Important: New, severe, or lasting chest pain needs urgent medical care.

How doctors assess: Symptom history, ECG, stress test, and sometimes angiography.

Chest Pain Type

Kind of chest discomfort.

Codes: TA = typical angina, ATA = atypical angina, NAP = non-anginal pain, ASY = no chest pain

  • TA: pressure/tightness with walking or stress, relieved by rest.
  • ATA: pain not classic, may be sharp, brief, or in unusual area.
  • NAP: chest pain likely from non-heart causes (muscle, acidity, etc.).
  • ASY: no chest pain symptoms.

How to know: Best decided by doctor after symptom interview.

Resting BP

Top blood pressure value while at rest.

Typical normal: around 90-120 mmHg (systolic)

Measured with: Digital BP monitor or manual sphygmomanometer + stethoscope.

Cholesterol

Total cholesterol in blood.

Desirable: below 200 mg/dL

Measured with: Blood lipid profile test in lab (venous sample).

Fasting BS (0/1)

Fasting blood sugar flag.

Enter: 0 if 120 mg/dL or below, 1 if above 120 mg/dL

Measured with: Glucometer (finger prick) or fasting plasma glucose lab test.

Resting ECG

Heart's electrical pattern at rest.

Options: Normal, ST (possible abnormality), LVH (possible heart muscle thickening)

Measured with: 12-lead ECG machine.

Max Heart Rate

Highest heart beats per minute reached during exertion.

Common formula: roughly 220 - age

Measured with: Treadmill stress test ECG or heart-rate monitor/smartwatch.

Exercise Angina

Chest pain/discomfort during exercise.

Options: Yes or No

How to know: Symptom history during walking/exercise, often confirmed in stress testing.

Oldpeak

ECG ST depression during stress test.

Typical normal: close to 0 (higher values may indicate risk)

Measured with: Exercise ECG/stress test report.

ST Slope

Direction of ECG ST segment during exercise.

Options: Up (often better), Flat or Down (can be concerning)

Measured with: Interpreted by clinician from stress ECG tracing.

Note: These are general reference values for awareness, not a diagnosis.

Before You Fill: Diabetes Terms Guide

Simple meanings and common adult ranges. Enter your recent medical values when possible.

Pregnancies

Number of times pregnant.

Typical range: 0 and above

How to know: Personal medical history (no device).

Glucose

Blood sugar level (usually fasting).

Typical fasting normal: about 70-99 mg/dL

Measured with: Glucometer or fasting plasma glucose blood test.

Blood Pressure (BP)

Bottom blood pressure value (diastolic).

Typical normal: about 60-80 mmHg

Measured with: BP monitor (digital/manual cuff).

Skin Thickness

Triceps skinfold thickness, related to body fat estimate.

Common dataset range: around 10-50 mm

Measured with: Skinfold caliper by trained health worker.

Insulin

Blood insulin concentration.

Typical fasting reference: around 2-25 mu U/mL (lab-dependent)

Measured with: Fasting insulin blood test in lab.

BMI

Body Mass Index from height and weight.

Healthy range: about 18.5-24.9

Measured with: Weighing scale + stadiometer (height meter), then BMI formula.

Diabetes Pedigree Function (DPF)

Score representing family-history effect on diabetes risk.

Range: non-negative; higher means stronger inherited tendency

How to know: Computed by model from dataset; not directly measured by a device.

Age

Your age in years.

Typical range: 18-100 years

How to know: Self-reported from date of birth.

Note: Ranges are educational and may vary by lab, age, and personal condition.
